I've been writing an album of folk songs about AI. I've been inspired by Jesse Welles and heyday Bob Dylan and by the crazy, self-absorbed culture of Silicon Valley, about which I love much, but which I feel a deep duty to change. I think the thing that kills me most is that San Francisco could be the world's premier public servant: tech is just power, and it's the power to change the world, and we could change the world in so many good ways by building things people need. *We could be the most popular guy in the room.* Instead SF is locked in an insane money-fueled AI race, might upend the rest of the unfathomably big and diverse world, and if you talk to most individuals, they're *still* just trying to do their own small job, scale themselves, make money. Really good people, people I love, are part of this system and can't seem to figure out what to do about it.

My message to the techies of the valley is: look over at the rest of the country. Go talk to some of them. They're *scared of us.* They are *trembling* wondering what'll happen with AI, even as they use it because they have to, and because it's addictive. There's a race on right now, but collectively *we've already won:* software has eaten the world. We're like a father flying about in a frenzied rage who doesn't realize he's hurting his family, and they're scared of him. It's time to calm down, to come to, to see their huddled and trembling faces, to try to apologize.

I've found that folk songs are somehow a really powerful way to get through to people. Sharing these songs with people, I've found that I can get across in one song what woulda taken a half hour to say worse. Of course, there are already people writing folk songs about AI, but honestly, I think if your audience is Silicon Valley, you need to know it from the inside. I'm an insider: I've been employed by an AI startup for the past four years. I've seen what Silicon Valley gets right, and I've seen what it gets wrong. It's really good at building, at doing, at discipline, at personal growth and management; it's full of folly when it comes to people and the complexities of society, which it always reduces to cartoons. It's hard to build something that actually benefits someone *as a person* when you only refer to them as a "user" and only see them through engagement statistics.

This is even my criticism of EA and Anthropic: at least they want to do *good,* but their understanding of people-as-apes (as opposed to people as rational beings) is pretty underdeveloped, so you get ideas like "oh, Anthropic employees giving people some of their wealth will counteract none of them having jobs," which I've literally seen an Anthropic soon-to-be-billionaire offer as a solution onstage in front of hundreds.

I hope to do as much good as I can with my actual scientific research, but a bitter lesson I've accepted lately is that I might actually be able to do more with my voice. At the least, I should do both.

So this first song I'll share is the Technocapitalist AI-Powered Blues. It's a long one. It was really hard to get all those lyrics back in one take! In the song I talk about four tech CEOs. Here's what I really think about them.

Mark I really can't understand: anyone who's gone on Facebook in the last five years knows how horrendously it's been colonized by ads and content farms and political ragebait. These changes are driven by algorithm design. Surely Mark could make a choice any day to (a) give up lots of ad money and (b) improve the experience of billions of people. It's crazy to me that he doesn't. Like, don't you have enough money already? I can't imagine empathy for so many people, and the consequent guilt, not eating at me til I do that. I'd like to see him do that.

Like many, I was a big fan of Elon in the early SpaceX/Tesla days. I wanted to work at SpaceX at one point. It does seem to me like he's pretty traumatized and is making up for a lack of good-feeling in life by going all-out on all of his projects. I admire his hustle and ability to cut through red tape. However, it does seem like he's lost touch with the world, and people, and what people actually need and want. Buying X just to make it worse seemed like a vanity project with major public consequences, and his relationship with Trump and all the DOGE stuff made it impossible for me to remain a fan. If Silicon Valley could be the world's premier public servant collectively speaking, I think Elon could be the world's premier public servant individually, a beloved hero and immensely popular figure. I bet he'd really like that. He could probably still do it.

Sam Altman I was also a big fan of early on, honestly. I wish I could have worked for him in the early days of OAI, before there were real stakes. He seems like a remarkable leader, even if everyone around him is constantly blowing the whistle on him. For me, the tipping point was the DoW incident, in which he seemingly couldn't resist a chance to win and thus gave up a real chance to present a united Silicon Valley front with red lines around AI weapons. Made me feel like the whole culture of OAI was about ruthless opportunism with a layer of talk on top.

Dario is the only one of the four I have actual admiration for as a person, not just as a business leader. Anthropic's also the only big AI lab I could see myself working for. See my above criticisms of Anthropic and EA --- for sure they're focused on this cartoon of "safety" and "the good future" without a real sense of what a good future for us apes actually looks like, and they tend to stare so hard at their own navels that it must hurt their necks. (This is not a novel criticism, and I know many folks in these worlds who'd agree.) Anthropic also has cognitive dissonance baked into it ("Sam's on a power-hungry spree / that's why you should give all the power to me!"). Nonetheless, he's the only one who's clearly taken actions that sacrifice revenue in exchange for following principles. It's small, but it's better than average. Even if it won't stop me from songwriting about the many valid criticisms of Anthropic, I'd like to see a lot more of that self-sacrifice.

Without further ado, here are the lyrics for the Technocapitalist AI-Powered Blues.
