Networks trained by plain SGD (muP, no normalisation, no biases) on grayscale CIFAR-10 to predict a fixed random label per image (10-way cross-entropy on 10,000 images): no information about the images is in the target. Score: for each 16 × 16 first-layer unit \(w\), the excess kurtosis of \(u = w\cdot x\) over 100,000 crops of the input the network sees (whitened or raw). Gaussian = 0; the Gabor-like units of sparse coding and ICA score 2.4-3.4 on these patches. 930 of 1093 runs finished.

  • Yes: a tiny CNN learns localised Gabors from random labels. conv 16×16 (16 filters) → |·| → average-pool to 6×6 → hidden 512 → 10 random labels on 10,000 whitened images; plain SGD, batch 32, η 0.3, weight decay 10-4, L1 10-5 on the first layer, 100k steps. 81-100% of the filters are localised Gabors in nine of nine seeds, median excess kurtosis 3.7-4.7 (sparse coding on these patches: 69%, kurtosis 3.4). Without the L1 penalty 75-94% (eight runs); 32 or 64 filters 72-73%. Reference run (James, 2026-10-30): the same net on 50,000 images, batch 128, 160k steps, first-layer rate ×3 (nn7_r_cnn_combo_N50000_pool6_C16_abs_l1_lr1x3): 15 of 16 localised, kurtosis 4.71, the default on tab b. Note there is no relu anywhere: the hidden layer's nonlinearity is |·| too. Pictures on tab d.
  • What is required (each removal tested): whitened input, |·| rather than relu (relu needs 200k steps and gets 81% at kurtosis 2.6), the hidden layer, a pooled map with 4-6 px cells, at least 10 classes, about 100k steps, weight decay. Without decay the filters become Gabors as the labels are fitted and then drift back to gratings.
  • Coarser pooling gives gratings, not Gabors: pool 4×4 or a global average yields oriented filters spanning the kernel with kurtosis 3-4 and 0% localised. Finer than 8×8 and the hidden layer memorises without training the filters.
  • It is the fitting, not the memorising: 50,000 images at 24-57% train accuracy still give 88-94% localised at kurtosis 4.5-4.7 (with the L1 penalty; without it, 0-38%).
  • MLP on 16×16 patches (16 units): a random label per image with fresh random patches each step gives heavy-tailed oriented gratings (kurtosis 3-5, 0% localised). A random label per (image, 4×4 position cell) on 3,000 images localises some of the units: over 45 runs of that setup (weight decay 10-4 to 10-3, η 0.3, 100k-200k steps, hidden 512-2048, seeds) the median is 25% localised, the middle half 19-31%, the best 62% (kurtosis 3.4-6.4). Averaging the |·| features over a bag of 8 patches of the same cell with decay 10-3 gives 25-56% over seven seeds (median 38%; 32 units: 41-50% over three); bags of 16-32, decay 10-3 without a bag, 32-64 units without a bag: worse. A random label per patch gives nothing: it is memorised through random features.
  • True labels with the same recipe: 81-100% localised. The constant target: never. The recipe transfers to ImageNet-64 (10,000 images, pool 12 for the same 5 px cells, η 0.1, whitening floor 10-3, decay 3×10-4): 69-94% localised over six runs with 16 filters (L1 on or off), kurtosis 5-7; 78% with 32 filters. Runs with fewer than 16 first-layer units are not shown (they are in results/).

why the units are gratings spanning the kernel, and what makes them localised: the pooled map's resolution

Purpose. The high-kurtosis units of this page are oriented gratings that span the 16 × 16 kernel, not the localised Gabors of sparse coding. James's hypothesis (2026-10-29): the filter is applied at every position and pooled, so a filter that tessellates is as good as a localised one. Localisation here is measured by the Gabor fit (fraction of units that are localised Gabors: oriented, envelope at most a quarter of the kernel), as a description, not as the score.

  • The plain recipe and what weight decay does. conv 16×16 (4 or 8 filters) → |·| → pool 6×6 → hidden 512 → 10 random labels on 10,000 whitened images, batch 32, η 0.5, weight decay 10-4 to 3×10-4: 75-100% localised in every seed (nine of nine at 4 filters, decay 3×10-4 best). Over training nothing happens for 10k steps; between 10k and 30k, as the labels start to be fitted, the filters become Gabors (kurtosis 1.2 → 4.5); without weight decay they do the same and then drift back to gratings by 100k steps (25% → 0% localised). The decay keeps the Gabor. Batch 128 or η 0.3: 50-75%; all 50,000 images at 4 filters: no fit, no Gabors.
  • Update (nn7 / nn8): at pool 6 with 16 channels, giving the first layer a learning rate ×3 and an L1 penalty of 10-5 takes localisation from 62-75% to 81-94% (three seeds, envelope 0.18-0.19), kurtosis 3.7-4.5; on 50,000 images 94% at 4.7; with true labels 88%. Learning rate ×10 alone at pool 8: 69%; ×30 diverges. 20,000 images: 88%; 2,000-5,000: 44-50%. Max pooling: tighter envelopes (0.13) but fewer localised units (50% at pool 6). 32 channels 72%, 8 channels 38%; hidden 256 75%, 128 50%; η 0.1: 0%, η 1 diverges; no weight decay 31%; decay 10-3 kills the fit at 16 channels.
  • What it takes: |·| units and a pooled map whose cells are about the size of a Gabor. On 32 × 32 images the conv map is 33 × 33; pooling it to 6 × 6 or 8 × 8 (cells of 4-6 px) gives 38-56% localised Gabors (envelope 0.20-0.24 of the kernel; sparse coding on these patches: 69%, 0.13) at median kurtosis 2.8-3.3. Pool 4 (8-px cells): 5% with 64 channels, 44% with 16; pool 2 or a global average: 0%, gratings. Pool 10-12: 3-17%; pool 16 and no pooling: 0% and back at the kurtosis baseline: on a fine map the hidden layer memorises the 10,000 images without training the first layer (the filters move 5-35% of the way).
  • Breaking translation equivariance does not localise, it removes the pressure. Stride 2: 1.6; stride 4-16 (the kernel laid down as tiles): 1.0-1.1, the first layer barely moves. No padding: 1.0-1.9.
  • Fewer channels localise more (16: 44-56%, 64: 5-45%); a gentle L1 penalty on the first layer (10-5) adds a little (pool 8: 44% localised at kurtosis 3.4), 10-4 zeroes the layer. A bigger hidden layer (2048) keeps 45%; a 64-unit one under-fits (3%).
  • relu instead of |·|: 0% localised at every pool size. Low-pass whitening (floor 10-3, 10-2) and biases did not help.
  • James's picture, amended: pooling to coarse energy maps makes a tessellating grating as good as a Gabor, so the net takes the grating (higher frequency, more kurtosis). Give the hidden layer where at a 4-6 px grain and a localised filter becomes worth having. Take away the pooling and nothing in the first layer needs to be learned at all.
