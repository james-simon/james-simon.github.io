Toward a science of intelligence

We aim to develop a first-principles, quantitative science of how intelligence is learned, represented, interpreted, computed, and organized in brains and machines.

Research pillars

The Redwood Center's research agenda has five interrelated pillars.

  1. 1

    Learning mechanics

    What features and representations are learned through gradient-based training?

    We work out how architecture, data, task and learning rule together determine what a network learns: which features emerge during training, in what order, and whether simple laws govern the dynamics.

    Read more: learningmechanics.pub

  2. 2

    Structure of data and learned representations

    How does the structure of the data drive the structure of representations?

    We ask how symmetries, statistics and latent variables in data shape the geometry of what networks learn, and when that geometry reflects real structure in the world rather than quirks of a particular model.

  3. 3

    Principles of mechanistic interpretability and neural computation

    What are the right mathematical objects for describing computation inside a neural network, biological or artificial?

    We look past individual features toward manifolds, transformations and latent variables as ways to describe what trained networks compute, drawing on decades of work on how brains represent the world.

  4. 4

    Dynamics of computation

    How does intelligent computation unfold at test-time, in LLM reasoning traces, diffusion models, RNNs, and biological systems?

    We treat computation as something that happens over time, and follow how information is transformed, stored and accumulated as a system iterates, in the brain and in models.

  5. 5

    Collective intelligence

    What new behavioral laws or organizational principles emerge when many intelligent agents interact?

    A new direction for the Center: we study populations of interacting AI agents, looking for scaling laws, phase transitions and other collective behavior that no single agent would predict.
